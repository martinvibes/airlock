# Airlock video: three storylines

Facts used on screen come from the repo (README, docs/BLOG.md) and the Nov 5 live run screenshots. KudiMart and Paylane are fictional, and all data is synthetic.

## A. "The door" (recommended, picked)

Why first: a stranger follows two companies blaming each other, a wall going up between them, and a door that only lets typed claims through. Everyone has seen that fight.

- **Device and form:** one object across worlds (a claim ticket, then the number 37) in an object-to-object chain. Each shot ends on the thing that opens the next: the wall becomes the door, the door becomes the room plan, a chat message becomes the ticket.
- **What the viewer sees and feels:** two coloured rooms, KudiMart in clay and Paylane in teal, with an oud wooden door between them (the logo; it stands for Customs). Things try to cross. Free text gets stamped REFUSED, the investigator bounces off with a 403, and only a released claim slides through. The viewer laughs at the bounces and cheers when the 37 go through.
- **First two seconds:** a receipt card fills the left of the frame, "KudiMart · order KM-20417 · ₦18,500". A green PAID stamp slams onto it, then a clay PENDING stamp slams over it. "37 customers paid." lands huge on the right.
- **Beats:** hook (paid, never arrived) → blame (97 vs 42, "your bug") → wall (logs are customer data) → reveal (the wall grows a door: Airlock) → rooms (engineer, investigator, Customs, and the 403) → claim (real Mattermost: propose, queued, refused, released, through the door) → halves meet ("two compounding causes") → match (480 blinded dots, 443 cancel, 37 left) → replay fails (401) → fix and payoff (37 delivered, 0 stuck, incident closed) → claim ("The walls are Switch") → end card (the door opens on the wordmark).
- **Look:** cream plaster ground, oud wood for walls and doors, company colours as room floors, paper claim tickets with hard offset shadows, rubber stamps. Archivo Black, Outfit and IBM Plex Mono.
- **Sound:** "Funkorama" (Kevin MacLeod, CC BY 3.0) at 101 BPM. The quiet intro carries the hook. The band enters on the blame. The breakdown (104 to 121 s) sits under the 37 and the failed replay, and the drop lands on "37 delivered". Effects: stamp slams, ticket whooshes, typing on claims, an error buzz on every refusal, pops on numbers, chimes on delivery.

## B. "Whodunit"

- **Device and form:** type carries the argument, cut on the beat, like the reference reel: giant kinetic words and hard colour floods change every half beat.
- **What the viewer sees and feels:** a case file. "THE CASE OF THE 37 MISSING ORDERS." Suspects (the canary, the rotation) get mugshots in giant type, and clues arrive as typed claims. It's fast and funny, and it works on mute.
- **First two seconds:** a clay flood, then "37" fills 80% of the frame width and "ORDERS VANISHED" slams under it.
- **Beats:** case opens → suspects → the evidence locker (can't share logs) → the detective agency (Airlock) → clues cross → the reveal (two compounding causes) → the count → case closed → end card.
- **Look:** flat colour fields (clay, teal, oud, cream) and League Gothic at 60 to 90% of the frame width. No UI except one Mattermost punch-in.
- **Sound:** a faster funk track, every cut on the beat, and stamps on clues.

## C. "A day in the war room"

- **Device and form:** a moment in someone's day as one continuous take with timestamps, mostly real UI.
- **What the viewer sees and feels:** 15:54, KudiMart's engineer types the question in Mattermost. The camera never cuts. It travels from the war room through the door to airlock and to Paylane's room, following the clock to 16:01, incident closed. It's calm and credible, and it feels like watching over their shoulder.
- **First two seconds:** the real Mattermost channel, a timestamp "15:54" in the corner of the frame, and the engineer's question typing in.
- **Beats:** the question → the investigator's answer → queued → released → Paylane's half → match → replay fails → fix → closed → end card.
- **Look:** the product's own UI at 1.6 to 2×, on a cream desk ground with a timestamp ticker.
- **Sound:** a low lo-fi bed with keyboard and notification sounds as the main texture.
